THE PIPELINE

From raw news events to a shaded map

Five automated steps run on every cycle.

01

Pull the latest events

The bot downloads the most recent window of global events from GDELT 2.0, an open dataset that refreshes every 15 minutes with no usage restrictions.

02

Keep only physical conflict

Only CAMEO root codes 18 Assault, 19 Fight and 20 Mass violence are kept. Everything else (diplomacy, protests, statements) is dropped.

03

Add fatality data when available

When an ACLED connection is configured, its recorded events are merged in. ACLED is the only layer that carries fatality estimates.

04

De-duplicate and store

Events are normalised and de-duplicated by source and ID so the same incident is not counted twice across runs.

05

Recompute country statistics

Rolling 7, 30 and 90-day counts, an intensity score for each country and a global summary row are refreshed. The map reads these, not the raw feed.

KEEPING IT A WAR MAP

Three filters between the feed and the map

READING THE SHADING

Intensity is relative, not absolute

0 · QUIET50100 · HIGHEST IN THE WORLD
intensity = 100 × (country events in last 30 days) ÷ (highest country total in last 30 days)

A country scores 100 only because it has the most recorded events right now. When another country overtakes it, scores shift for everyone. A country is flagged active once it passes 5 events in 30 days.

BE CLEAR-EYED

What the map shows, and what it never will

It gives you

  • Where qualifying events happened
  • How frequently, over 7, 30 and 90 days
  • Fatality estimates, where ACLED data is connected
  • A relative intensity ranking between countries
  • A separate verified-intel layer that is curated by hand

It never invents

  • Wounded counts
  • Property-damage dollar values
  • Territory-control claims
  • Anything marked verified that a person has not checked
  • Precision beyond what the underlying news data supports

HOW THE DATA IS COLLECTED

Open data, processed by rules

Unlike the other features, the Conflict Map doesn't use a language model to write anything. It's a data pipeline with fixed rules.

WHERE THE DATA COMES FROM

GDELT 2.0

An open database of news-derived events, updated every 15 minutes. The main source for locations and frequency.

ACLED (optional)

Recorded conflict events with fatality estimates, merged in when connected.

Verified intel layer

A separate table curated by a person for details automated sources can't provide reliably.

WHAT THE ANALYST DOES, STEP BY STEP

  1. 1

    Download the latest window

    Every 3 hours the latest window of GDELT events is pulled.

  2. 2

    Keep conflict codes

    Only Assault (18), Fight (19) and Mass violence (20) events remain.

  3. 3

    Require an armed actor

    At least one side must be a military, rebel, insurgent, separatist or other armed group.

  4. 4

    Remove noise

    Countries where those codes mostly reflect domestic crime and policing are excluded, and duplicates are dropped.

  5. 5

    Recompute the statistics

    7, 30 and 90-day counts, intensity scores and the global summary are refreshed for the map.

No invented numbers

Because the map is rule-based, it can only report what the sources contain. Wounded counts, damage values and territory control are never estimated.

Limitations

  • The source is news coverage. Under-reported regions can look calmer than they are, and heavily covered ones can look worse.
  • Automated location and actor tagging can be wrong. A handful of events may be mis-assigned or duplicated.
  • Event counts measure how often incidents are reported, not how severe they are.
  • The map is context for risk awareness, not a trading signal. See the Risk Disclosure.
