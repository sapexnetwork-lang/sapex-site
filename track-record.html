THE DEFINITIONS

Four words, counted the same way every time

Every signal ends in exactly one of these. The month and batch numbers on this page are sums of them.

Released

A signal the AI published. It is counted in the month it was released (UTC), whatever happens to it later.

Success (win)

The price reached the take-profit before the stop-loss and before the time limit.

Failed (loss)

The price reached the stop-loss first.

Expired

Neither level was reached within 72 hours, or it was retired because more than 70 signals were open at once.

WIN RATEwins ÷ (wins + losses)Expired signals are not in this figure. The page shows them separately and also shows the stricter rate that counts them as losses.

HOW THE NUMBERS ARE COLLECTED

From a released signal to a number on this page

No one types these figures in. They are produced by the same bot that releases the signals.

WHERE THE DATA COMES FROM

The signal bot

It releases each signal with an entry, a take-profit and a stop-loss, and later records how it ended.

Live market prices

Crypto prices come from an exchange feed and stock prices from a market data feed. They are compared with each open signal about every 15 minutes.

A permanent ledger

Each signal is written once to a separate ledger file. It is keyed by its own id, so it can never be counted twice.

Static public files

This page reads small data files. Visiting it does not touch our database.

THE LIFE OF ONE SIGNAL, STEP BY STEP

  1. 1

    Released

    The signal is created with its entry, take-profit and stop-loss and counted as released.

  2. 2

    Checked

    About every 15 minutes the live price is compared with its two levels.

  3. 3

    Closed as a win or a loss

    The first level reached decides it. The result and the price at that moment are saved.

  4. 4

    Or expired

    If no level is reached within 72 hours, or the open limit of 70 is exceeded, it expires.

  5. 5

    Numbered and frozen

    A win or a loss gets the next closing number. Every 100 numbers a batch is sealed with a fingerprint code.

  6. 6

    Published

    The files are rebuilt from the ledger and published at most once an hour, and only when a number changed.

Only finished results are public

Open signals stay inside the Terminal. This page never shows a live entry, so it cannot be used to copy a signal that is still running.

Limitations

  • A win rate is not a profit. This page counts how often a target or a stop was reached, not how large each win or loss was.
  • Prices are checked about every 15 minutes, not on every tick. A short spike between two checks can be missed, and a result can be recorded slightly past its target.
  • Expired signals are not in the win rate. If they were counted as losses the rate would be lower, and the page shows that figure too.
  • The ledger starts at the first date shown at the top. Nothing earlier is included.
  • All dates and months are UTC. Signals from the current month can still be open.
  • Past results do not predict future results. Nothing here is financial advice.
