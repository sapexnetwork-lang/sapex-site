Where GTA 6 Stands Two Days After Launch

Grand Theft Auto VI went on sale on November 19, 2026, for PlayStation 5 and Xbox Series X and S. No PC version has been announced. According to Output Lag's review roundup, the standard edition costs $79.99 and the Ultimate Edition costs $99.99. Today is November 21, so the game has been in players' hands for only two days, and every figure in this article is an early reading that can still move. The headline number is a Metacritic score of 99 out of 100. Latin Times and TechPowerUp both report that this ties The Legend of Zelda: Ocarina of Time for the highest Metascore ever recorded, and it sits above GTA IV's 98. This article collects what critics, players, performance testers and sales analysts have reported so far, and it flags where sources disagree or where the data is thin. ## What Critics Are Saying TechPowerUp's snapshot of the PlayStation 5 listing counted 147 critic reviews, 118 of them perfect scores, with no mixed or negative reviews at that moment. Output Lag, checking Metacritic on November 20, counted 153 reviews. The Xbox Series X listing also showed 99, but from only 14 reviews, so it is a much smaller sample. Counts differ between snapshots because new reviews keep arriving, and OpenCritic has been reported at either 98 or 99 depending on the day. Perfect scores came from outlets including IGN, GameSpot, Game Informer, Edge, Eurogamer, GamesRadar+ and Video Games Chronicle. Not every score was perfect. TechPowerUp lists GameStar at 97, Wccftech at 96, The New York Times at 90, IGN Japan at 90, and ScreenHub at 80, the lowest on the list. ScreenHub's reviewer said the game drags for a long stretch before it opens up. The praise follows a few clear themes. Output Lag's summary of its sampled reviews points to a dense, reactive open world, the story and performances of the two leads, Jason and Lucia, the soundtrack and radio stations, and weighty combat. Gulf News reports that, in a sample of 18 reviews, 16 praised the two leads and 9 praised the music. The same report says six of those 18 reviews found the realism systems frustrating, and reviewers also noted a slow opening and uneven pacing. ## What Players Are Talking About Critic scores are not the same as player experience. In the sources we could check, no aggregated player score was available. One guide citing Metacritic's page showed the user score as "tbd" with zero ratings, but that figure was dated October 19, before launch, so it is out of date. That means the picture of player opinion below comes from social media posts and community discussion, which tends to amplify the loudest voices in both directions. Gulf News reports that players are enjoying how the world reacts to them. The six-star Heat system uses surveillance cameras, so escaping police may mean changing clothes and cars. Activities such as fishing, golf, hunting, diving, bird watching and drone races are spread across the map. Players also describe the driving as heavier than in recent games in the series. A clip of a dog in goggles steering a jet ski went viral, and a new character named Mandy, absent from the pre-launch marketing, has sparked theories. There is real pushback too. IBTimes UK reported on a single early-access player who gave the game 2 out of 10, praising the graphics but criticising performance, the story, and what he called filler activities. The article describes this as one personal opinion, not a coordinated review campaign, and fans replied that four hours is too little time to judge a story-driven open-world game. The Post Millennial describes player reactions as mixed and quotes complaints about bugs and about routine tasks feeling like chores. Both pieces show a gap between critic consensus and a vocal minority, but neither provides a measured user score. ## Performance and Technical Problems Rockstar capped the game at 30 frames per second on all consoles, and Gulf News reports that Rockstar confirmed this on November 10, with no performance mode. Digital Foundry's analysis, reported by Video Games Chronicle, found that the base PlayStation 5 can fall below 30 frames per second during normal play, such as driving around the city. Those dips were described as very minor but noticeable, and they became sustained when the testers caused as much destruction as possible. Digital Foundry called the PlayStation 5 Pro the best way to play. It held a near flawless 30 frames per second, dipping to 24 in the worst stress tests, and John Linneman said he played more than 50 hours on the Pro outside stress tests without a single dip. Gulf News adds that the Xbox Series S is the weakest version, with reports of frame rates under 20 and a 225GB download, though these are reported figures rather than official ones. Bugs have also been reported. Latin Times lists crashes during the prologue, a progress-blocking softlock in an early Lucia mission, intermittent screen freezes, characters falling through the ground and unstable vehicle physics. It says Rockstar has not publicly addressed these. PC Guide argues that the launch is nowhere near as broken as Cyberpunk 2077's was in December 2020 and that early reports do not suggest most players are affected. Separately, Latin Times reports PlayStation Network disruptions on November 18 and November 19, the second lasting roughly 24 minutes, which Sony acknowledged on its status page without giving a cause. ## Sales Estimates and What They Mean for Investors Take-Two Interactive has not released official sales figures, so every number here is an outside estimate. Sensor Tower estimated about 17 million copies and more than $1.65 billion in revenue by November 18, as reported by GGRecon and attributed to Reuters. Fox 5 New York reports a Sensor Tower estimate of about $1.84 billion by release day, including months of pre-orders, with about 75 percent of sales on PlayStation. For comparison, Fox 5 notes that GTA V earned $815.7 million in its first 24 hours in 2013, a record listed by Guinness World Records, and sold 11.21 million units in that period. Latin Times adds that Oppenheimer estimated $1.85 billion in revenue over the first three days of sales, though the article does not say how those days are counted, and that Wells Fargo analysts projected about 65 million units sold in Take-Two's fiscal year through March 2027. These are analyst projections and can be wrong, and pre-order revenue is not the same as ongoing demand. For readers who follow Take-Two as a stock, the useful points are the size of the estimates, the platform split, and the fact that the publisher's own numbers have not arrived yet. This article is not financial advice, and nothing here says how any share price will move. ## Sources This article draws on reporting published between November 18 and November 20, 2026 by Output Lag, TechPowerUp, Latin Times, Gulf News, PC Guide, IBTimes UK, The Post Millennial, Video Games Chronicle, GGRecon and Fox 5 New York. Figures are as those outlets reported them on those dates, and several are third-party estimates. Scores, review counts and sales numbers may have changed since.
