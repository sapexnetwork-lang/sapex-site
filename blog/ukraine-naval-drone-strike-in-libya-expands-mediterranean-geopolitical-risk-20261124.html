The Situation & Verified Facts

Ukrainian naval drones struck Russian vessels docked at the Mediterranean port of Benghazi, Libya, over the weekend, causing a Russian warship to explode and sink [1]. The operation, conducted thousands of kilometers from the main theater of the war in Eastern Europe, marks a significant geographical expansion of maritime engagements between Ukrainian forces and Russian naval assets [1]. The Tripoli-based foreign ministry of Libya has formally condemned the operation, while Russia has raised diplomatic accusations against neighboring NATO members regarding operational support [1] [2].

According to reports published today on November 24, 2026, Ukraine confirmed that its security service, the SBU, deployed seven domestically built naval drones launched from a mothership to carry out the strikes in Benghazi [1]. The strikes resulted in the total destruction of the Russian corvette Merkury, which exploded and sank in the port, while also hitting a dry cargo vessel and damaging two cargo ships that Ukrainian officials stated were being used to transport military hardware [1] [2]. Medical officials in Benghazi reported that ten individuals, including both Russian and Libyan personnel, sustained minor injuries and received medical treatment following the explosions [2]. Ukrainian President Volodymyr Zelenskyy characterized the deep-water operation as exemplary, citing the targeted destruction of logistics capacity supplying Russian military efforts [1].

The foreign ministry of Libya UN recognized government based in Tripoli publicly condemned any foreign military actions conducted within Libyan territory or territorial waters and requested a formal explanation from Kyiv [1]. Concurrently, Moscow issued a formal diplomatic protest after summoning the charge d'affaires of Estonia, accusing the Baltic nation of allowing Ukrainian drones to utilize its airspace secretly to facilitate attacks against Russian maritime infrastructure [2]. Estonia's foreign ministry issued a statement on Monday denying all allegations, reaffirming that the nation has never permitted and will never permit foreign powers to use its sovereign territory for military operations, while Moscow also accused Estonia of attempting to hinder Russian cargo traffic across the Baltic Sea [2]. Meanwhile, broader political discussions within Ukraine continue regarding potential strategic directions, where controversial statements by parliamentarian Oleksiy Goncharenko advocating for potential peace negotiations resulted in his expulsion from his parliamentary faction in October after he argued that Ukraine cannot defeat Russia militarily [3].
