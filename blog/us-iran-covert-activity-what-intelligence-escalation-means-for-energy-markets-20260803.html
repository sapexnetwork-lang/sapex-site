US-Iran Covert Activity: What Intelligence Escalation Means for Energy Markets

Aug 3, 20262 min read

Public diplomatic negotiations between the United States and Iran are currently underway, but beneath the surface of official talks, intelligence analysts forecast a very different reality. Analysts assess a 70% probability that US intelligence agencies, including the CIA, are escalating covert operations against Iranian targets. Despite public announcements of diplomatic engagement, underlying tensions remain severe, and analysts indicate that a lasting peace is still far off. This dual-track environment—balancing public diplomacy with covert pressure—is an established pattern, where intelligence operations are actively deployed beneath the surface to disrupt adversary capabilities and build strategic leverage.

The projected escalation targets critical elements of Iranian state power, focusing on Iranian nuclear facilities, Revolutionary Guard (IRGC) leadership, and key economic infrastructure. These covert efforts build upon a long history of US-Iran intelligence conflict and reflect a continuation of the US maximum pressure policy framework. According to the intelligence summary, the overarching purpose of intensifying these covert actions is to weaken the Iranian regime, disrupt its nuclear and ballistic missile programs, and exert maximum pressure to secure more advantageous terms in future negotiations. These operations are already active, with potential escalation forecasted over a 6 to 18-month timeline.

Because this assessment is probabilistic—carrying a 70% estimated likelihood rather than absolute certainty—traders must evaluate the situation as a risk factor rather than a confirmed outcome. Covert intelligence operations operate out of public view, making direct real-time tracking challenging for market participants. However, the secondary impacts of covert friction often spill over into open market dynamics. Heightened covert engagement increases the probability of unexpected geopolitical friction, which can undermine diplomatic progress without warning or trigger retaliatory responses from the Iranian regime and the Revolutionary Guard.

For market participants, the primary impact of escalating US-Iran covert operations centres on heightened asset volatility and shifting risk premiums. Energy markets are particularly sensitive to ongoing geopolitical friction in the Middle East. Intensified covert operations contribute directly to oil price volatility and expand the risk premium priced into Middle Eastern assets. Additionally, covert activity carries the threat of cyber-attacks that could impact critical energy infrastructure. As these dynamics develop over the 6 to 18-month timeline, staying informed on geopolitical developments remains crucial for managing market exposure.
